## Q1

Suppose $L$ is context-free and let $p$ be its pumping length. Take $s=a^pb^pc^pd^p\in L$. Consider **any** decomposition $s=uvxyz$ with $\lvert vxy\rvert\le p$ and $\lvert vy\rvert\ge1$. To meet three blocks, $vxy$ would have to contain an entire middle block and at least one symbol on each side, a total of at least $p+2$. Hence it meets at most two adjacent blocks.

Pump down with $i=0$. Some letter in $vy$ is deleted. Its required partner (a with c, or b with d) lies in a nonadjacent block and cannot occur in $vy$. Thus the deleted letter now occurs fewer than $p$ times and its partner still occurs $p$ times. This gives $uxz\notin L$ for every legal decomposition, contradicting the CFL pumping lemma.

## Q2

A deterministic two-tape TM is a tuple $M=(Q,\Sigma,\Gamma,\delta,q_0,q_a,q_r)$ with finite state set $Q$, input alphabet $\Sigma\subseteq\Gamma$, finite tape alphabet $\Gamma$, blank $B\in\Gamma\setminus\Sigma$, initial state $q_0$, and halting accept and reject states $q_a,q_r$. For $D=\{L,R,S\}$ (left, right, stay), its transition function is

$$
\delta:(Q\setminus\{q_a,q_r\})\times\Gamma^2\longrightarrow Q\times\Gamma^2\times D^2.
$$

Both tapes are infinite in both directions. Initially tape 1 contains the input with its head at the first cell, tape 2 is blank with its head at the corresponding cell, and all remaining cells are blank.

Take $\Sigma=\{a,b\}$ and $\Gamma=\{a,b,B\}$. The states are start (initial), odd, even, back, cmp, acc (accept), and rej (reject). Each pair is in tape order. Instantiate each row for $t\in\{a,b\}$. Every omitted nonhalting entry preserves both symbols and enters rej with both heads stationary.

| State | Read (1,2) | Next | Write (1,2) | Move (1,2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| start | t,B | odd | t,t | R,R |
| odd | t,B | even | t,t | R,R |
| even | t,B | odd | t,t | R,R |
| even | B,B | back | B,B | L,S |
| back | t,B | back | t,B | L,S |
| back | B,B | cmp | B,B | R,L |
| cmp | t,t | cmp | t,t | R,L |
| cmp | B,B | acc | B,B | S,S |

The first three transitions copy the input and track its length. Only a positive even length permits entry to back. In cmp the heads start at opposite ends, comparing the original from left to right with its copy from right to left. Both heads reach blank together after all mirror pairs match. Thus the machine accepts exactly the positive even-length palindromes. Splitting such a palindrome in half gives $ww^R$ with $w\ne\varepsilon$, and every such $ww^R$ passes the test. Each scan is finite.

## Q3

Use one two-way infinite tape with alphabet $\{0,1,U,V,X,B\}$, blank B, initial state q and halting state h. Start at the first input bit. Set M(0) = U and M(1) = V. These marked symbols record finalized bits. X marks the left endpoint of the current interval. In the table, i and j range independently over 0,1, m over U,V, and k over U,V,B. Subscripts are part of finite state names. The displayed rules suffice on every input in the domain.

| State | Read | Next | Write | Move |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| q | i | S_i | X | R |
| S_i | j | S_i | j | R |
| S_i | k | E_i | k | L |
| E_i | j | C_j | M(i) | L |
| C_j | i | C_j | i | L |
| C_j | X | q | M(j) | R |
| E_i | X | A | M(i) | L |
| q | m | A | m | L |
| A | m | A | m | L |
| A | B | D | B | R |
| D | M(i) | D | i | R |
| D | B | H | B | L |
| H | i | H | i | L |
| H | B | h | B | R |

Each round remembers the leftmost unprocessed bit i, replacing it with X. At the rightmost unprocessed bit j, the machine writes M(i), carries j left, and writes M(j) over X. This fixes the two mirror positions and leaves the interior intact. If only the center remains, E_i reads X and restores M(i). For an even-length input, q eventually reads a marked bit. In both cases A goes to the left edge, D removes all marks, and H positions the head on the first output bit. Each round removes two unprocessed positions or finishes the center. The machine therefore halts with exactly $w^R$ on the tape.

## Q4

Let $C=\{a^nb^n\mid n\ge0\}$, generated by $S\to aSb\mid\varepsilon$. Let $R$ be the regular language described by $a(aaa)^{\ast}b^{\ast}$.

If $s\in C\cap R$, then $s=a^nb^n$, and membership in $R$ gives $n=1+3k$ for some $k\ge0$. Hence $s\in K$. Conversely, if $s\in K$, its two blocks have equal length, so $s\in C$. Its a-block has length $1+3k$ for some $k\ge0$, so $s\in R$ as well. Thus $K=C\cap R$ is context-free by closure of CFLs under intersection with regular languages.
