What connects these six objects?

The geography of American manufacturing is better understood by distinguishing where work occurred from who controlled its connections. Across these six cases, ownership, exchange, contracts and coercive labor relations crossed production locations in different ways; distance alone does not describe a progression from local work to globalization.

    Different places, different responsibilities

    Across the six cases, a location identifies work but does not by itself identify control. Ownership, exchange, coercion and contracts connect places differently. The RCA map shows selected factory roles, while the iPhone map offers a separate supplier-office layer. Their apparent spread is not a like-for-like measure.
