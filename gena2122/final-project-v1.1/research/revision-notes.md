Revised 5 November 2026 in response to four fresh, sequential Claude Code reviews of frozen v1 against the GENA2122 rubric. The main reviewer returned model identifier `claude-opus-5-5`. It reviewed text, not the running website; v1.1 is the editor's revision, not a Claude-certified release.

The shared argument now distinguishes where work occurred from who connected it through ownership, exchange, contracts and coercive labor relations. Six cases challenge different assumptions about a production address; they do not describe a uniform advance from local to global production. A comparison matrix sets out the selection logic and each case's evidence window.

- **Saugus:** colonial privileges, English capital, Scottish prisoners' indenture and the venture's eventual failure qualify a purely technical account.
- **Lowell:** the 1835 public railway opening differs from the still-developing merchandise arrangements described in an 1836 account. Slavery is part of the economic relationship. The compilation's clothing category does not establish a fiber recipe or a same-bale return journey.
- **Armour:** Chicago processing and a California retail advertisement are separate observations. The advertised cans' factory and metal source remain unknown; no traced shipment is claimed.
- **Ford:** 1928 assembly, later safety-glass arrangements and Richmond's 1931 opening retain their dates. Integration distinguishes work at one site, ownership across sites and external suppliers.
- **RCA:** announced spending, capacity forecasts, existing tube production and planned tooling are separated. Company plants supplied competitors; a firm's network does not identify the components of an individual set.
- **iPhone:** the optional supplier-office layer is off by default. Named offices are not factories. The argument acknowledges concentrated outsourcing and the limits of evidence about wages and individual assemblers.
