Course connections

The core comparison draws on the history sequence in Lectures 1–6. Lecture 7 supplies an additional source-reading method. Lecture references are course-context references; the object-level claims depend on the outside sources identified in each case. Original Blackboard files are retained locally.

1650 — The iron nail

Lecture 2, pp. 12–13: British colonies and commercial ties. Saugus supplies an independently researched material example. Lecture 1, pp. 25–26 informs the distinction between mapped evidence and inference.

1835 — Cotton cloth

Lecture 3, slides 4–6: sectional identity and slavery. Lecture 4, slide 4 offers a later comparison with southern textile industrialization; it is not evidence about Lowell in 1835.

1901 — Canned beef

Lecture 4, slides 6–7: rail expansion, concentrated capital and industrial labor. Slide 15 supplies the later context of food regulation; the 1901 case predates those reforms.

1928 — The Model A

Lecture 5, slide 10: automobiles and manufacturing in the 1920s. Slide 12 places northern industrial jobs within the Great Migration; it does not identify the workers in the photographed assembly line.

1965 — Color television

Lecture 6, slide 8: postwar suburban growth and changing regional opportunities. RCA's particular factory network comes from outside research; the lecture does not establish these plant assignments.

2007 — The first iPhone

Lecture 6, slide 19: globalization and the growing importance of internet devices. The 2007 phone extends that context through independently sourced evidence; a separately dated 2025 epilogue provides the present-day connection.

Method across the exhibition

Lecture 1, pp. 25–26 asks what maps include or omit and how spatial patterns might be explained. The atlas applies those questions to role-based maps with explicit gaps. Lecture 5, slides 18–20 asks for a debatable thesis, deliberate primary/secondary source use and contextual interpretation of primary materials. Lecture 7, slides 15–16 develops close reading of sources including advertisements and images.
